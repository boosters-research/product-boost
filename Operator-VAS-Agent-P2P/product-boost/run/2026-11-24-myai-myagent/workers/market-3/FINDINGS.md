## Answer

Contract-level gross margin for a telecom VAS contract or a banking voice-agent contract is **UNPUBLISHED**. CAC payback in months for either population is **UNPUBLISHED**.

The 1.5× CAC buffer is the user requirement. It cannot be applied, because no opened source states the CAC or the unbuffered payback. Buffered payback months are **UNPUBLISHED**. A 12-month window and a 50% gross-margin floor were not used as criteria. K5 is not marked clear.

What is published is company-level gross margin for listed telecom-content, telecom-messaging, and contact-center or voice-AI firms. None of those figures is the margin on one operator contract or one bank voice-agent contract. Where a percent is derived rather than labeled by the filer, it is marked DERIVED.

## Pricing and WTP

Gross margin is the metric. Willingness-to-pay prices were not collected.

OnMobile's two gross-margin readings are both kept. They use different revenue denominators.

- Presentation, year ended 31 Mar 2026: gross profit INR 2,763 Mn / gross revenue INR 5,245 Mn = 52.7%. FY25: INR 2,726 Mn / INR 5,832 Mn = 46.7%. The deck says COGS includes UFF and does not define UFF.
- Audited results, same date: revenue from operations INR 5,168.50 Mn. Content fee and royalty INR 2,295.48 Mn + contest expenses INR 31.27 Mn + cost of software licenses and others INR 157.12 Mn = INR 2,483.87 Mn. Residual INR 2,684.63 Mn. Ratio 2,684.63 / 5,168.50 = 51.9%. The statement does not call this gross margin. India was INR 834.43 Mn of that revenue, which is 16.1%. The margin is not an India-only contract margin.
- The same audited statement records an impairment of INR 468.40 Mn on a receivable from one party whose revenue contract was terminated in the year. That is a contract loss, not a gross margin.
- The chairman's letter, in the gaming paragraph, says product margins reached 11% by the fourth quarter, against a long-term objective of about 25%. Marketing expense was 53% of gaming subscription revenue in FY26, down from 65% in FY25. The entertainment and communication business "generated INR 3,700 million in revenue while maintaining a 21% product margin," with the same about-25% objective. The letter does not define product margin. These percents are not the 52.7% gross margin.
- The INR 1,545 Mn gaming figure is not usable as a growth rate. The annual report says gaming subscription and gaming platform revenues of INR 1,545 Mn grew 38%. The presentation says mobile gaming revenue of INR 1,545 Mn fell 25.4%, and separately says gaming subscription revenue grew 32.2% without tying that rate to the INR 1,545 Mn. Levels do add: 1,545 + 3,700 = 5,245, the presentation's gross revenue. The growth rates conflict (E-MKT-043).

Tanla, year ended 31 Mar 2026, states gross margin 26.6% (FY25 26.1% in the same table). Gross profit INR 11,746 Mn / revenue INR 44,177 Mn = 26.6%. Definition in that letter: revenue less cost of services, and cost of services is the transaction fee paid to mobile carriers and other suppliers. Digital platforms were 8.9% of revenue: INR 3,949 Mn revenue and INR 3,888 Mn gross profit. 3,888 / 3,949 = 98.5% DERIVED. Residual enterprise communications: revenue 44,177 − 3,949 = 40,228; gross profit 11,746 − 3,888 = 7,858; 7,858 / 40,228 = 19.5% DERIVED. The platform definition in the same letter says only cloud and hosting are a charge on platform revenue, which is why the residual is high. That residual is not a voice-agent contract margin.

Sinch, year 2025, key-data gross margin 34.8%. 9,426 / 27,080 = 34.8%. The report says this measure is not defined under IFRS. Gross profit is net sales less cost of services sold, and a large share of that cost is traffic fees paid to mobile operators. Regional gross margins in the same report: Americas 35%, EMEA 32%, APAC 40%. Sinch's "acquisition costs" line is the cost of a business combination, not customer acquisition cost.

Five9, year ended 31 Dec 2025, 10-K: gross profit $632.854 Mn / revenue $1,149.088 Mn = 55.1%, shown as 55% in the MD&A. The 19 Feb 2026 earnings release states GAAP gross margin 55.1% and adjusted gross margin 62.8%. The 10-K says professional-services offerings typically have negative margins and usage revenue has lower margins, so 55.1% is a blend. It also says the customer base includes banking and financial services and that no customer was more than 10% of revenue in 2025, 2024, or 2023. It does not state the banking share.

NICE, year ended 31 Dec 2025, 20-F: total gross profit 66.4% of revenue ($1,956.1 Mn / $2,945.4 Mn). Cloud gross profit 65.6% of cloud revenue ($1,467.9 Mn / $2,238.4 Mn), stated in the filing. Services 65.4%. Product 83.1%. Selling and marketing was $661.1 Mn, 22.5% of revenue. The filing warns that AI inference and compute costs are significant and volatile and may reduce gross margins. It does not give a number for that effect, and it does not give a banking voice-agent margin.

SoundHound, year ended 31 Dec 2025, 10-K: gross margin 42%, against 49% in 2024. Gross profit $71.551 Mn / revenue $168.920 Mn = 42.4%, which the 10-K rounds to 42% and the 26 Feb 2026 earnings release states as 42.4%. The 10-K attributes the decline primarily to amortization from the Amelia acquisition ($14.1 Mn in 2025) and the Interactions acquisition ($0.8 Mn). Non-GAAP gross margin in the earnings release is 58.0%, after adding back intangible amortization of $17.224 Mn and stock-based compensation and related payroll taxes of $9.194 Mn. Non-GAAP is not the GAAP gross margin.

LivePerson, year ended 31 Dec 2025, 10-K: revenue $243.742 Mn; cost of revenue, exclusive of depreciation and amortization, $69.392 Mn; depreciation and amortization $22.732 Mn, presented separately and not allocated. The 10-K does not state a gross-margin percent. A ratio that ignored that exclusion would be an invented label, so none is published here. Revenue was $401.983 Mn in 2023 and $312.474 Mn in 2024. The customer list includes telecommunications and financial services. No revenue split is given. CAC payback is not stated.
